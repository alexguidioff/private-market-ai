## Abstract

The research foundations of Chapters 4–12 are the point of the programme; this chapter describes how
they could translate into products, a company, and a funded academic path — deliberately *after* the
science, not before. It sketches the product evolution (from an uncertainty-first valuation assistant
to a private-market operating system), the moats, the Swiss market and funding ladder, and the entry
paths into research. A disclosure note is essential: this chapter is the least suitable to show
academics early (§13.7).

---

## 13.1 Product evolution

From `Startup_Ideas.md`, the productisation mirrors the research arc:

```
V1  Valuation Assistant (uncertainty-first)  — ranges + reasons + value of information (Ch.10)
V2  Due Diligence Copilot                     — grounded agentic diligence + memo drafting (Ch.9)
V3  Investment Copilot                         — sourcing + screening + valuation + DD + IC support
V4  Private Market Operating System            — the decision-intelligence platform (the "Bloomberg")
```

Each version is a productised layer of the architecture (Chapter 12) and depends on the corresponding
research being done first.

## 13.2 The differentiator

Every competitor outputs a number; the wedge is the **decision object** of Chapter 10 — a fair-value
range with confidence, the reasons behind it, what is missing, and what it is worth finding out. This
is honest about the residual uncertainty the founding paper is explicit about, and investors trust
ranges-with-reasons more than a single confident figure (`Startup_Ideas.md §0`).

## 13.3 Moats

1. **Proprietary data & feedback** — beyond licensed sources: user feedback, uploaded documents,
   decision outcomes recorded in the Decision Graph (Chapter 6).
2. **Representation & integration** — the hard-to-copy layer (Chapter 4, §4.8; Chapter 7, §7.2).
3. **Network effects** — every fund that uses the system improves the shared representation and the
   decision-quality models.
4. **Verticalization** — owning "AI for venture capital / private markets", not a generic tool.

## 13.4 Market and segments (Switzerland-first)

Switzerland is dense with buyers: VCs, growth funds, family offices, private banks, PE funds, wealth
managers, and corporate VC — with high willingness to pay for B2B tools and large tickets (UBS,
Julius Baer, Pictet, Lombard Odier entering private markets). Segments, in likely order of entry:
VC/growth funds → family offices → corporate VC → private banks (possibly via API). *(From
`Startup_Ideas.md §4–5`.)*

## 13.5 Academic entry paths (prerequisite to funding)

Funding requires a host first, then the grant (`Funding.md`). Entry modes, from
`Entry_Paths.md`, ranked for this profile: **Research Assistant / Research Engineer** (paid,
immediate, data access) → collaboration → grant-based research → (optionally) industrial PhD.
The due diligence in `Group_Funding_DD.md` identified **SDSC** (actively hiring engineers) and
**Ademi / Borth (HSG)** as the strongest Wave-1 entry points; the department of any eventual PhD
matters for career labelling (`Career_Options.md §5`).

## 13.6 Funding ladder

From `Funding.md` (amounts indicative — verify):
- **BRIDGE Proof of Concept** — research→innovation; needs an academic host.
- **Innosuisse Innovation Project** — applied research with an industry implementation partner (a VC,
  family office, bank, or data provider — a natural fit for §13.4).
- **ETH Pioneer Fellowship / EPFL Innogrant / UZH Entrepreneur Fellowship** — translation, host-lab dependent.
- **Venture Kick** — later, once there is a Swiss company and an MVP.
Sequencing: host (RA/collaboration) → grant → spin-off. Not the reverse.

## 13.7 Disclosure: keep this chapter for later

This is the chapter *least* suited to early academic outreach. Some professors are wary of candidates
who "just want to build a company"; leading with commercialization can undercut the research
credibility that opens doors (Chapter 1, §0.1; `Outreach_Brief.md`). The rule:
- **To professors, now:** the paper and Paper #2 (representation). Not this chapter.
- **To applied-grant evaluators (BRIDGE/Innosuisse) and, later, investors:** this chapter is exactly
  what they want to see.
Match the artifact to the audience.

## 13.8 The long arc

```
Amazon → WI2026 paper → Swiss research group (RA / Research Engineer)
      → 2–3 papers (#2–#4) → BRIDGE / Innosuisse → spin-off → Private Market AI company
```

The company, if it happens, is the *last* step and rests on everything before it: a real result, a
represented domain, a decision theory, and a funded research base. That ordering — science first,
company last — is the strategy, not an accident of sequencing.
