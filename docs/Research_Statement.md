Private capital markets — venture capital, growth and private equity, family offices — move
trillions of dollars on the basis of scarce, heterogeneous, and highly uncertain information.
Unlike public markets, they lack a shared, machine-reasoning-ready representation of the world.
My research asks a deceptively simple question: **how should AI represent private companies, and
support the investment decisions made about them under uncertainty?**

**Background.** My accepted WI2026 Student Track paper (with S. Rashid and H. Zhong), *Can
Non-Financial Signals Price Private Companies?*, studies startup valuation with interpretable
machine learning. Using 3,403 PitchBook deals and GEM macro indicators in a progressive
three-layer design, and a strict out-of-time holdout across the 2022 market correction, we show
that ML models trained *only on non-financial signals* (firmographics, deal context, investor
syndicate) price private companies competitively with the best financial baselines — and more
robustly across regimes — while adding financial and macro data yields negligible improvement.
SHAP analysis identifies investor-syndicate capacity, not firm financials, as the dominant pricing
driver. That result — that structure and context can substitute for financials — is what shaped
my research direction.

**Research vision.** The valuation result convinced me that the harder and more fundamental
problem is not prediction but **representation**. Before we can rank, value, or run due diligence
on a company, we need a structured model of it: its entities, relationships, events, and
behavioral signals, evolving over time. My longer-term aim is to build the scientific foundations
of **decision intelligence for private capital markets** — treating valuation as the first use
case rather than the destination, and treating uncertainty (ranges, confidence, missing
information, value of information) as a first-class output rather than an afterthought.

**Current focus (next step).** My immediate project is a **Private Market Data Model**: a unified,
temporal, multimodal representation of private companies and their ecosystem, designed to be
reasoning-ready for downstream AI. This connects naturally to explainable ML, knowledge graphs,
and representation learning, and it is where I am seeking methodological feedback and
collaboration.

**Three research directions I would like to develop.**
1. **Representation** — a structured, unified model of private companies (entities, relationships,
   events, signals) as the substrate for decision support.
2. **Uncertainty-aware decision support** — distributional, explainable outputs that make
   explicit what is known, what is not, and what is worth finding out.
3. **Human-AI decision-making** — how AI can *participate* in investment reasoning (screening,
   diligence, committees) without replacing human judgment.

**What I bring.** A rare combination for this problem space: applied ML (Python, scikit-learn,
SHAP, temporal validation), a business and entrepreneurial-finance background (ESCP), and
industry experience as a Product Manager at Amazon — plus a peer-reviewed publication in the
area. I am comfortable both building systems and reasoning about the decisions they support.

**What I am looking for.** A Swiss research group with which to turn this work into a coherent
research line — through collaboration, a Research Assistant / Research Engineer role, or a jointly
developed project (e.g. BRIDGE / Innosuisse). I am especially interested in groups working on
explainable AI, representation learning, decision-making under uncertainty, or entrepreneurial
finance and private markets.
