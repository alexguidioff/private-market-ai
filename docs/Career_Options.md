## 1. PhD vs. separate research — sequencing
- The goal is not "a PhD or nothing". Preferred order (see `Entry_Paths.md`):
  **RA / Research Engineer first → informed PhD decision after 6–12 months.**
- A European PhD is a **paid job** (ETH/EPFL doctoral salary ≈ CHF 50–70k+/yr) and a
  **cumulative dissertation** = 3–4 coherent papers, not one.
- What to bring:
  - **First conversation / RA:** only **Paper #2** (representation). Disclosure discipline.
  - **Formal PhD proposal (4 yr):** Paper #2 in detail + #3/#4 in *decreasing depth*, framed as one
    question in layers. Not #5/#6 (only a one-line "long-term horizon"). No startup/commercial part.

## 2. US vs. Switzerland
**Recommendation: stay with Switzerland** for the PhD.
- US PhD: 5–6 yrs (incl. coursework), brutally competitive (1–3% at top schools), rigid Dec deadlines,
  weak fit for an industry profile with few academic references, and it throws away the Swiss
  ecosystem we mapped (15 groups, BRIDGE/Innosuisse, VC network, RA-first path).
- Switzerland: paid well, ETH/EPFL are top-10 globally, non-linear entry possible, your network is here.
- **US makes sense later** — as a *post-PhD* move into **industry** (not an academic postdoc).

## 3. Post-PhD fallback (if the research topic itself goes nowhere)
The value of a PhD is transferable skills, not the topic. With an ML/CS PhD:
- Quant / ML in finance (banks, hedge funds, asset managers — UBS, Pictet, quant funds).
- Applied/Research Scientist in industry (incl. returning to Amazon far higher; Google/Meta/etc.).
- Technical partner / data lead inside VC/PE funds.
- Consulting / corporate development.
- **Real risk of a PhD is opportunity cost (4 yrs at doctoral pay), not unemployment.**

## 4. Salaries (indicative, verify)
- **US academic postdoc:** ~$55–70k (Bay Area/NYC ~$65–80k) — economically weak vs. Swiss PhD.
- **US industry research (Research/Applied Scientist) with a PhD:** ~$200–400k+ total comp; top AI
  labs higher. Industry "postdoc" (MSR/Google Research) ~$150k+.
- **Takeaway:** if maximizing income post-PhD, US pays a lot — via **industry**, not postdoc. An
  ETH/EPFL PhD opens those doors as well as a US PhD.

## 5. PhD department = career label (decisive for the plan B)
The department on the PhD is a first-pass filter for recruiters, even if the research is the same ML.
- **CS / ML / Information Systems PhD** → "ML researcher" → Applied/Research Scientist, AI labs ($$$).
- **Finance PhD** → "economist who uses ML" → quant/finance academia; starts behind for AI-lab roles.
- Mapping to Wave-1 targets:

  - **Krause (ETH, D-INFK)** → purest ML label ✅✅
  - **Gonon (HSG, CS + FSI)** → ML-in-finance, CS-based ✅
  - **Tykvová (HSG, Finance)** → Finance label ⚠️
  - **Ademi (HSG, ITEM)** → Management/innovation label ⚠️ (great co-author/mentor though)
- **Best structure: CS-based PhD (Borth/Gonon/Krause) + finance co-advisor (Tykvová/Ademi)** →
  "ML" label + private-markets credibility.

## 6. Engineering degree (personal goal) — do it AFTER, cheaply and online
A PhD does **not** grant an engineering degree. Decision: pursue it as a *separate, later* project,
for the credential/personal-goal reason. Constraints: prestigious, fast to convert an
economics BSc + management MSc, **budget ~£3–5k**, no re-doing a bachelor.

### Prestige (verified 2026-07)
- **Georgia Tech OMSCS — genuinely prestigious.** Georgia Tech is a top-10 US CS school; the OMSCS
  diploma is **identical to the on-campus MSCS** (does not say "online"). Cited by Harvard Kennedy
  School and Columbia as the model that combined low-cost online delivery with a highly-ranked
  degree. Well respected in tech. **Winner on prestige.**
- **University of London — solid but a step below** in tech reputation; reputable distance-learning
  awarding body. ⚠️ **City, University of London (online) states there is currently no formal
  accreditation** for its programmes — check accreditation before choosing that route.

### Credit conversion — the key trade-off (verified 2026-07)
- **Georgia Tech OMSCS — almost NO conversion.** Graduate transfer credit is capped at ~**6 credit
  hours** (of 30), i.e. 1–2 courses at most, and typically only for **graduate-level** courses
  already taken — not undergraduate economics exams. So with your background you'd convert little
  and take ~10 courses. The low total (~$7k) comes from the **low per-credit price (~$180)**, not
  from skipping courses. You largely do the whole degree.
- **University of London — YES, conversion exists (their strength).** Formal **Recognition of Prior
  Learning (RPL)**: if you hold qualifications with learning outcomes similar to their modules, you
  can apply and *"will not need to study those modules"*. Your quantitative background (stats/math
  from economics) may exempt some modules.
- **Do you still pay per converted credit?** ⚠️ Usually **partly yes**: most universities charge an
  RPL assessment fee, and the headline price does not drop proportionally to exempted modules — RPL
  mainly saves *time*, not necessarily the full cost. Confirm per programme.

### Prestige vs. conversion — the tension
| | Prestige | Credit conversion | Real cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Georgia Tech OMSCS** | High (top-10, "real" MSc) | Minimal (~6 cr max; needs graduate courses) | ~$7k, done almost in full |
| **University of London** | Good | **Yes, via RPL** (module exemptions) | £3–5k diploma band; full MSc more |

**There is no option that is simultaneously top-prestige, heavily-convertible, and ~£3k.** Pick the
axis that matters most: prestige → OMSCS (~$7k, full degree); speed/budget/conversion → UoL + RPL.

### Timing
- **Not in parallel with the early PhD** (years 1–2 are all-consuming; risk of diluting papers).
- Use the PhD's *required graduate coursework* to take CS/engineering courses (substance + credits,
  though not a separate degree).
- Do the formal online MSc/diploma **later** (PhD year 3–4 when writing-heavy, or after), when you
  can also leverage PhD credits.
