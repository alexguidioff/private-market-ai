Can Non-Financial Signals Price Private Companies?

A Machine Learning Approach to Startup Valuation
Alessandro Guidi, Saad Rashid, Hao Zhong (ESCP Business School)  ·  Accepted, WI2026 Student Track

Problem

Private startups are hard to price: extreme information asymmetry, non-linear growth, and short histories make DCF impractical and comparables unstable during market swings. Can non-financial signals substitute for traditional financial metrics in startup valuation?

Data

  • 3,403 venture-capital deals from PitchBook, plus macro data from GEM (Global Entrepreneurship Monitor).
  • Out-of-time holdout spanning the post-2022 correction: train on pre-April-2022 (bull, n=2,723), test on post-April-2022 (contraction, n=680) — a genuine regime shift for all models.

Method — a progressive 3-layer design

  • Layer 1: OLS financial baselines (Revenue, EBITDA, Revenue Multiple).
  • Layer 2: ML on non-financial features only (firmographics, deal context, investor syndicate).
  • Layer 3: ML on the full set (Layer 2 + financials + macro) — an ablation to measure the marginal value of financial data.
  • Models: Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, ElasticNet. Interpretability via SHAP.

Results (out-of-sample holdout, n=680)

ModelR²MAE
OLS Revenue Multiple (best financial baseline)0.6430.895
Random Forest — Layer 2 (non-financial only)0.5570.888
Gradient Boosting — Layer 2 (non-financial only)0.5580.897
Gradient Boosting — Layer 3 (full set)0.5520.901
  • Non-financial ML is competitive with the best financial baseline — the Layer-2 Random Forest achieves the best absolute error (MAE 0.888).
  • The OLS Revenue Multiple shows a higher R² (0.643) but the highest cross-validation instability, generalising poorly across regimes; the ML models are more robust.
  • "Information saturation": adding financial and macro data (Layer 3) yields virtually identical performance to non-financial-only (Layer 2) — trailing financials add minimal value.
  • SHAP: the collective capital capacity of the investor syndicate (mean AUM, ~31% of explained importance), not firm financials, is the dominant pricing driver — a certification effect.

Contribution

  • Evidence that non-financial signals alone can price private companies competitively — and more robustly across market regimes — than financial baselines.
  • A progressive, interpretable 3-layer framework that quantifies the marginal value of financial data (here, largely redundant).
  • A foundation for the deeper question: which information is worth acquiring to support an investment decision under uncertainty.
