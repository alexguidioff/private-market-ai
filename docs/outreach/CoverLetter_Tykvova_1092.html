I am writing to apply for the Research Associate / Ph.D. Student position in Private Markets at the Swiss Institute of Banking and Finance. I am an ML researcher with a background in private-market valuation, an accepted publication in the area, and a clear wish to pursue an academic-oriented PhD under your supervision — the position describes almost exactly the path I want to build.

My interest in private markets is concrete rather than abstract. My paper Can Non-Financial Signals Price Private Companies? (accepted, WI2026 Student Track; with S. Rashid and H. Zhong, ESCP) studies 3,403 PitchBook venture deals under a strict out-of-time holdout across the post-2022 correction. Two findings connect directly to your research programme. First, the dominant driver of pricing is not firm financials but investor-syndicate capacity (the collective AUM of the lead investors accounts for ~31% of model importance) — an empirical confirmation of the certification effect, and a result that speaks to your work on syndication and selection in venture and private equity. Second, adding financial and macro data to a purely non-financial model yields negligible improvement ("information saturation"), which raises a question I find genuinely open: which information is actually worth acquiring before a private-market decision, and at what cost?

That question is what I would like to develop into a doctoral research line: bringing the economics of the value of information into private-market investment decisions, with the empirical rigour your Chair is known for. Crucially, the work does not depend on proprietary data — a point-in-time reconstruction of public sources (excluding look-ahead, in the spirit of the out-of-time design I already use) combined with a virtual-fund simulator provides a testable ground truth. I would value the empirical private-markets environment of your Chair as the home for this, and I am open to shaping the topic within your group's ongoing research agenda.

I work in Python (scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP) with strong empirical-analysis skills — tree ensembles, regularised regression, temporal validation, and interpretability — and I have professional experience at Amazon and Capgemini that keeps my work close to practice.
