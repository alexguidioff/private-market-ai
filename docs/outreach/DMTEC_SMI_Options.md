### Honest fit assessment

**Where it matches.** They want someone moving between research, engineering and the human/institutional
consequences of AI systems — reliability, accountability, human-AI interaction "as research questions in
their own right, not deployment afterthoughts". That is close to **P3 (human-AI at the investment
committee)**, and the emphasis on high-stakes settings, user studies, and building prototypes that take
their real-world context seriously fits an Amazon/PM-plus-applied-ML profile better than any pure theory
group. They ask for a master's in "computer science, machine learning, HCI, **or a related field**".

**Where it does not match — state this plainly.** The domain is **money systems and democracies**, with
trustworthy AI voting assistants as the named example project. It is *not* private markets. Taking this
would mean **working on their agenda, not P1.** The methodological thread (trustworthy human-AI decision
support in high-stakes settings) genuinely transfers; the empirical domain does not.

They also want hands-on LLM depth — evaluation, prompting, tool use, retrieval-augmented workflows,
adaptation, plus MLOps and frontend/backend as pluses. The Neural Academy LLM certification and the
WI2026 pipeline support part of that; production LLM engineering is thinner ground.

## How D-MTEC admission works (differs from D-INFK)

- Admission requires a university diploma or Master's **recognised by ETH**. Recognition checks go to
  the **central** administration: `doktorat@ethz.ch` — *not* the D-MTEC office, which states it does not
  handle applications or admissions.
- **You must find a professor willing to supervise.** Applications go directly to that professor. The
  doctorate normally involves employment at their chair.
- There is **no departmental portal application** to file speculatively, unlike D-INFK. No professor,
  no doctorate.

**The practical consequence:** at D-MTEC the bottleneck is a person, not a form. That is why the HiWi
route matters — it is a way to become known to a chair that currently has no open doctoral seat.
