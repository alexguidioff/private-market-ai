# D-MTEC chair screen — where does the research question actually live?

**Started:** 2026-07-29 · **Method:** read each chair's *research* page, not the chair title
**Purpose:** determine whether ETH D-MTEC contains a home for cost-aware value of information under
a capacity constraint, as an alternative or complement to D-INFK (where applications were submitted
on 2026-07-29).

---

## The screening criterion, fixed before scanning

Chair titles mislead. "Entrepreneurship" sounded like the closest fit and turned out to be
methodologically incompatible. So each chair is judged on three things read off its research page:

1. **Information as an object of choice** — is *which information to acquire* a question the group
   asks, rather than information being a generic resource?
2. **Method compatibility** — formal, quantitative or computational, versus qualitative and
   process-oriented. Not a quality judgement; a question of whether the existing apparatus has
   interlocutors.
3. **Empirical setting** — do private markets, investment decisions, or comparable
   costly-information decisions appear anywhere in the group's agenda?

**All three missing → no, regardless of how well the title fits.**

## Detailed notes

### ⭐ Information Management → Agentic Systems Lab — Fleisch & Wortmann

**Site:** `agenticsystems.ethz.ch` · **GitHub:** `github.com/Agentic-Systems-Lab` ·
**Contact:** `agenticsystems@ethz.ch` · **Founded:** July 2025

The Chair of Information Management runs three labs: Bits to Energy Lab (2007), Centre for Digital
Health Interventions (2013), and the **Agentic Systems Lab (2025)**. The third is the relevant one and
it is the first place in this entire screen that passes all three criteria.

**Why it passes on setting.** Research area 01 is agentic AI applications "from insurance and
**finance** to education and scientific research". The team page lists dedicated researchers by
vertical, including **Agentic AI in Finance** (Joseph Xavier Mertz Echauri), Agentic AI in Insurance
(Connor Larson, Jakob Flunger), and Agentic AI for Regulatory Affairs (Francesco Cavalli). Finance is
an active, staffed vertical — not a passing mention.

**Why it passes on the information question.** Research area 03, "Next-Gen RAG", describes systems
that **autonomously plan and optimise retrieval** — AutoRAG configuring pipelines automatically,
MetaRAG orchestrating multiple strategies for grounded outputs. Deciding which retrieval strategy to
run, under cost, *is* an information-acquisition problem. Research area 05 is **AI Evaluation
Frameworks**: benchmarks and methodologies for performance, safety and reliability of agents. That is
the same discipline as preregistered gates and declared falsification thresholds.

**Why it passes on method.** Computational and empirical, publishing at CS venues. Robert Jakob's
OpenReview profile lists agentic AI, multimodal LLMs, predictive analytics and TSLMs, with a PhD under
Fleisch (2020–2025) and a research stint at Harvard's Statistical Reinforcement Learning Lab.
Publications include work on multi-agent reinforcement learning and LLM auditing.

**The decisive feature — the predoctoral track.** The lab advertises four tracks, and one is
described as *a predoctoral program designed to turn your thesis into a top-tier CS conference
publication with you as first author*. That addresses the exact gap in this profile: the degrees are
in management, the publication is at an IS venue, and what is missing is a first-author CS-venue
paper. There is no other channel found at ETH that targets that gap directly. The team page already
lists several **Pre-Doctoral Researchers**, so the track is populated, not aspirational.

The other three tracks: **AI Transformation** (work with industry partners), **AI Founder** ("turn
your thesis or semester project into a launchpad for your venture"), and **Affiliate Researcher** (for
PhD, postdoc and industry researchers — collaborative projects with autonomy and mentorship).

**Two structural advantages over the D-INFK route.**

1. **It solves the two-institution problem in one place.** Prof. Felix Wortmann is Scientific Director
   at the *University of St. Gallen*, and Fleisch holds appointments at both ETH and HSG. Several team
   members are listed as PhD Researcher **HSG**. `Research_Agenda.md` requires a domain co-advisor at
   HSG alongside methodological supervision — this lab already spans both institutions natively.
2. **It is new and growing.** Founded July 2025, running hackathons with Google DeepMind, Vercel and
   AWS, with Gemini and AWS credits for projects and an insurance research partner. A young lab hiring
   into a fast-moving area is a materially easier entry than a chair led by an ACM/IEEE Fellow whose
   response probability `Group_Funding_DD.md` rates as Low.

**Honest limits, stated before acting on this.**
* The lab's framing is *applied* agentic AI, not decision theory. Cost-aware value of information under
  a capacity constraint is not on its stated agenda; the overlap is with retrieval planning and
  evaluation, which is adjacent rather than identical.
* Predoctoral positions are usually salaried but junior, and typically not equivalent to a funded
  doctoral position. Verify terms, duration, and whether it leads to a PhD place.
* The AI Founder track is attractive given the commercial ambition, but pursuing it would put the
  research agenda second — the same trade-off already identified elsewhere.

**Recommended action.** Contact `agenticsystems@ethz.ch` about the AI Research predoctoral track and
the Affiliate Researcher track, framed on two things this profile actually has: an evaluation
discipline (preregistered gates, locked test cohorts, disjointness assertions) that matches their AI
Evaluation Frameworks area, and a finance vertical that matches an active staffed area. Do **not** lead
with value of information — lead with evaluation of agentic systems in finance, which is their language.

### 📌 TESS (inside the TIM chair) — parked as a methodological ally, not an application target

**Contact:** Dr. Axel Zeijen, Senior Researcher, `azeijen@ethz.ch` (named TESS contact — a proper
channel, not the professor directly)

**Decision taken 2026-07-29: do not email now.** Recorded here to be used *when writing the paper*,
because the overlap is methodological and would be most valuable at that point, not as an application.

**Why this group matters despite the domain mismatch.** TESS is the quantitative half of Brusoni's
chair (COLAB is the cognitive/experimental half). Its projects use patent text analysis, Stack Overflow
data, formal and agent-based models. And one project shares this project's central methodological
problem almost word for word.

**Ming Chen's project on technological shifts** develops an automated, patent-based framework to capture
*early signals* of technological shifts, motivated explicitly by the observation that "most approaches
rely on retrospective assessments, identifying radical shifts only after they have reshaped competitive
hierarchies," with the stated goal of **real-time early-warning indicators**.

That is the point-in-time problem in a different domain. They face retrospective-versus-real-time
detection on patents and firms; this project faces it on regulatory filings and investors. Same
methodological vice, different corpus. Anyone who has built a real early-signal framework has
necessarily confronted look-ahead contamination, disjointness of train and test units, and how to
define "what was knowable when" — which is exactly what `code/` implements and what
`experiments/EXP-002` had to correct after the 846-CIK overlap was caught.

**Axel Zeijen's project** develops formal and agent-based models of innovation, coordination and
adaptation — adjacent to the synthetic multi-world harness.

**Why not now.** The domain is genuinely theirs and not mine: firms, patents, platforms, defence
procurement. Private markets appear nowhere. An email today would be a methods conversation with no
concrete artefact attached, which is the weakest version of it.

**When to use this contact.** When the paper on the metric-versus-capacity-constraint question is
drafted, or when the point-in-time reconstruction is written up as a method rather than as
infrastructure. At that point the approach is specific and reciprocal: *here is how I handled
retrospective contamination in a different corpus, does it transfer to yours?* That is a peer exchange
rather than a request.

### Technology and Innovation Management — Prof. Stefano Brusoni

Two research areas: **TESS** (Technology Systems and Strategy) and **COLAB** (Cognition, Learning
and Adaptive Behavior). COLAB states its goal as understanding the cognitive mechanisms of adaptive
decision-making and learning, using qualitative, experimental, **neuroimaging** and computational
methods. Brusoni describes his own work as studying decisions involving trade-offs — exploitation
versus exploration, social versus business objectives. Earlier work of his used fMRI on expert
decision-makers.

**The key distinction.** This is decision research from the *cognitive* side: decision as a
psychological phenomenon measured in people, not as a formal optimisation problem.

**Therefore it is the home of P3, not P1.** `Research_Agenda.md` P3 — *Human-AI at the Investment
Committee* — asks how AI participation changes committee decision quality, and requires experiments
with subjects. That is exactly COLAB's method stack. The same agenda flags P3 as the highest
coordination-cost paper precisely because it needs subject access — which this group already has.

Cost of that route: the thesis becomes behavioural rather than methodological. P1's value-of-information
core would not be the contribution.

**Open position noted:** a Research Assistant post for COLAB on jobs.ethz.ch requires a strong
background in cognitive psychology — not a match for this profile, but evidence the group hires.

**Dead end to avoid:** the "Decision Navigator" in the TIM site navigation is a multi-criteria
decision-support teaching tool (originally the *Entscheidungsnavi*), not a research programme on
value of information. Not a hook.

### Integrative Risk Management and Economics — Prof. Antoine Bommier

Chair page: economics of **risk management**, integrative view of interdependence between risks,
novel approaches to evaluation and **mitigation**. Applications named: savings, portfolio choice,
climate policy. Risk types: financial, environmental, demographic. Associated with CER-ETH.

A PhD-positions document lists three research areas, the first being *Decision Theory: Risk and
Uncertainty*. Bommier's own work covers generalised life-cycle utility, temporal risk aversion,
preferences over temporal lotteries.

**Why the initial read was too optimistic.** I first ranked Bommier first because "misspecified
utility model" is one of the three candidate explanations for P1's negative result, and utility
specification is his tooling. But the chair page contains no mention of information acquisition or
value of information. His question is how to manage interdependent risks; the question here is which
information to buy before deciding. Utility theory is an instrument he commands, not his object of
study — so the pitch would be asking him to work on someone else's problem with his tools, which is
a weak basis for supervision.

## The structural finding

D-MTEC contains three cultures, not two:

* **CER-ETH side** — Bommier, Gersbach, Egger, Sturm, Köthenbürger: formal economics, mathematical.
* **Management side** — Clarysse, Brusoni, von Krogh: organisation and cognition theory.
* **Applied-computational side** — Fleisch's labs, in particular Agentic Systems: engineering-led AI
  research with industry partners, publishing at CS venues.

The initial conclusion after four chairs was that D-MTEC had no home for this work and that D-INFK was
the only viable route. **The Agentic Systems Lab overturns that**, and it was nearly missed because the
chair is called "Information Management", which sounded like digital health and IoT.

**Two revisions of my own earlier judgements, recorded so the pattern is visible:**
1. I ranked Clarysse first on the word "Entrepreneurship" in the chair title, then dropped him after
   reading the research page.
2. I recommended stopping the scan on grounds of low marginal value — one chair before the best find in
   it. The screen was working; my estimate of when to stop was wrong.

Both errors are the same shape: judging a unit by its label, and estimating a search's remaining value
before finishing it.

## Who the Agentic Systems Lab actually admits — 24 pre-doctoral profiles, read 2026-07-29

Collected because of a reasonable objection: *labs are entered after studying, as a PhD or postdoc, not
during.* That describes most academic labs correctly. It does not describe this one, and the team page is
the evidence.

All 24 "Pre-Doctoral Researcher" profiles were read. Degree subject is stated for only 11, so every count
below carries that caveat.

### The four facts that matter

**1. Zero doctorates.** No profile states a completed PhD. The lab uses separate labels — "PhD
Researcher", "Postdoc", "Dr." — for other members. "Pre-doctoral" means literally before the doctorate.

**2. Current master's students are the modal case.** Eleven are explicitly current students, ten at
master's level and one — Haseeb Raza at ELTE Budapest — still in his **final year of a bachelor's**.
Thirteen do not state a status, and several of those read as post-graduation professionals (Citadel,
Optiver, IBM, PwC, CERN backgrounds) without saying so.

**3. Most are not ETH students.** ETH is the hub, not the source. Home institutions include University
of Twente (two people, both doing their thesis at the lab), EPFL, Politecnico di Milano, NTNU, CODE
Berlin, ELTE Budapest, WU Vienna, UPC Barcelona, Princeton and Stanford. Note also that ETH email
addresses are issued to visiting students, so an @ethz.ch address does not imply ETH enrolment.

**4. There is a direct precedent for a non-technical background.**

### The three comparable profiles

**Maximilian Weigl — "Industry Applications of TSLMs"** is the closest precedent and the answer to the
"I am not any of those categories" worry. His stated background is *finance and strategy at WU Vienna*,
with experience across hedge funds, venture debt and international market analysis. **No technical degree
is claimed.** His contact is a WU Vienna student email, so he is at the lab while enrolled elsewhere. His
project is identifying high-value industrial applications and commercial AI strategy. If he qualifies, a
management master's with a published ML paper and a working pipeline is not out of range.

**Àlex Martí Guiu and Jakob Flunger** are both currently in ETH's **MSc Management, Technology and
Economics** — a management programme, in D-MTEC. Both arrived from technical bachelors (Data Science at
UPC; Computer Science at ETH) and both have engineering work histories. Hybrids, not pure technical.

**Connor Larson — "Agentic AI in Insurance"** blends quantitative training with finance practice:
Operations Research and Financial Engineering at Princeton, then management consulting, **private equity**
and data science, now a master's at ETH working with Zurich Insurance. The nearest profile to a
private-markets background.

### Two other findings worth acting on

**A cost-aware project already exists in the lab.** Lorenzo Steno (MSc Computer Science, University of
Twente, thesis at the lab) works on **"Reinforcement Learning for Cost-Aware Recursive Language
Models"**. That is cost-aware sequential decision-making about how much computation or retrieval to spend
— structurally the same problem as cost-aware information acquisition, with compute cost instead of
diligence cost. It is the strongest concrete hook found: the lab is already working on the *shape* of the
question, in a different currency.

**Only 3 of 24 profiles link any publications** (Bertrand, Hollbeck, Shakir), and none names an individual
paper. An accepted peer-reviewed paper is therefore above the median for this group, not below it.

### What this changes

The girlfriend's objection is correct about academic labs generally and wrong about this one. This lab is
built as a pipeline that takes people *during or just after* their studies, from many universities, and
produces a first-author publication. That is precisely the missing step in this profile.

What it does **not** change: these are almost certainly unpaid or credit-based arrangements. The
funding analysis above stands unaltered.

**Source caveat.** Six profiles carry no biographical content at all (Ramesh, Ameri, Mertz Echauri,
Riemer, Boltshauser, Goyal). Notably **Joseph Xavier Mertz Echauri — "Agentic AI in Finance"**, the single
most relevant person by topic, has an empty page and no findable public record. Worth asking about
directly rather than inferring.
