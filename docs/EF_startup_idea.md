# EF Startup Idea — Continuous Capital Formation Network

**Status:** exploration thesis, not a validated company  
**Initial program:** Entrepreneurs First — FORM (London, 3 months) + LAUNCH (San Francisco, 3 months)  
**Initial geography:** UK–DACH–Switzerland as a testing corridor; US expansion if validated  
**Product principle:** decision support and coordination first; transaction execution only after legal and regulatory review

## 1. Vision

Private-company financing still begins as an episodic campaign. A company approaches the end of its runway, prepares a deck and data room, requests introductions, contacts funds, repeats diligence and loses most information generated by passes and conversations. Investors describe broad mandates, but their current capacity, conditions and dependencies remain fragmented across CRM notes, inboxes and partners' memories.

The long-term vision is to make private-capital formation **continuous, permissioned and agent-native**.

Companies maintain a live capital state: what capital may be needed, when, acceptable instruments and terms, relevant milestones, reusable evidence and disclosure permissions. Capital providers maintain executable mandates: strategy, ticket, timing, constraints, lead/follower role, required evidence and conditional capacity. Authorized agents continuously identify feasible capital configurations and coordinate the parties when conditions become true.

```text
Company state
  × Investor mandate
  × Evidence and permissions
  × Terms and timing
  × Other conditional commitments
  = feasible capital pathway
```

The desired behavioral shift is:

```text
Before: Who do I know who is raising or investing right now?
After:  Which capital configurations are feasible now, and what would make others feasible?
```

This is economically a marketplace, but its object and market design differ from a conventional listing marketplace. It would organize future, conditional primary capital rather than merely advertise open deals or trade existing securities.

## 2. The new graph

The core asset would not be a static company directory or a social graph. It would be a **Capital Formation Graph**:

```text
Company      → needs       → Capital configuration
Investor     → can provide → Conditional capacity
Evidence     → satisfies   → Condition
Commitment A → unlocks     → Commitment B
Decision     → produces    → Observed reliability
```

Each edge should carry identity, permission, provenance, timestamps, expiry, version and observed response. The platform guarantees this process record—not the substantive truth of every company claim. Companies and evidence providers remain responsible for content.

The potentially defensible dataset is:

```text
condition declared
→ acceptable evidence defined
→ trigger observed
→ counterparty response
→ condition maintained, changed or withdrawn
→ diligence, terms, commitment or no action
```

This can reveal which combinations become financeable and which participants behave consistently. Gaming cannot be eliminated; history, provenance and versioning should make it more visible and costly.

## 3. Starting wedge: Reconsideration Agent

The first product is not the full network. It is a private workflow for investors that turns genuine `not now` decisions into monitorable conditions.

```text
Investor would reconsider Company X
if Condition Y is reached
before Date Z
with Evidence E.
```

The system records the original decision, monitors or receives authorized evidence, triggers a review and records the actual response. It does not promise investment, score investors publicly or automate an investment decision.

Why this wedge may work:

- it can begin inside one fund, avoiding an immediate two-sided-market cold start;
- it addresses lost pipeline and stale CRM records;
- it gives founders a concrete route back instead of generic follow-up;
- it creates the first condition–trigger–response edges needed by the larger network;
- it is consistent with the project's point-in-time information, provenance and decision-record research.

Why it may fail:

- many pass reasons may be diplomatic rather than actionable;
- conditions may move once achieved;
- funds may not revisit enough companies to justify a product;
- CRM vendors could implement the workflow;
- investors may refuse to expose meaningful conditions or accept response expectations.

If authentic conditions do not lead to real reconsideration, this wedge must be abandoned rather than protected by the long-term narrative.

## 4. What this is not yet

It is not currently:

- a validated protocol or network effect;
- an exchange or transaction venue;
- a guarantee of company information;
- an autonomous investment adviser;
- proof that better coordination produces better returns;
- a product validated by P1.

P1 supplies useful representation concepts—information state, point-in-time provenance, acquisition cost, abstention and decision records—but its first real selective SEC-history policy passed 0/15 declared gates. Buyer utilities and real investment-committee behavior remain unobserved. The startup thesis therefore requires independent commercial validation.

## 5. EF months 1–3 — FORM in London

### Objective

Determine whether there is one consequential, repeated behavior from which a large capital network could grow. Do not build a broad marketplace.

### Month 1 — founder-market fit and problem evidence

1. Find a complementary cofounder with strong product/engineering ability and willingness to work in regulated B2B infrastructure. Fintech or capital-markets experience is useful but not a substitute for speed and customer access.
2. Select one initial capital-provider segment: emerging/micro-VC, CVC, family office or small growth fund. Avoid mixing all segments.
3. Conduct interviews based on past cases, not hypothetical interest:
   - recent companies passed as “too early”;
   - exact reasons and evidence recorded at the time;
   - whether and how cases were later reopened;
   - CRM, email and partner-memory failure modes;
   - cost of missed or late reconsideration.
4. Recruit a small number of founder cases with explicit consent and genuine fundraising histories.
5. Map legal boundaries around introductions, compensation, investment advice, data sharing and success fees in the UK/EU and US. The first product should avoid transaction execution and success-based compensation until reviewed.

### Month 2 — concierge experiment

Run the workflow manually before building production software:

1. obtain authentic passed/parked cases from design-partner funds;
2. convert free-text reasons into structured, private `reconsider if` records;
3. agree on acceptable evidence, expiry and who can see each field;
4. monitor milestones or let founders submit permissioned evidence;
5. trigger reviews when conditions are met;
6. record response time, reopening, changed conditions and next action;
7. test one dependency such as `lead interest → follower review` if a real case permits it.

Use identity masking where appropriate. Do not transfer private information between parties without consent.

### Month 3 — narrow MVP and Investment Committee evidence

Build only what the concierge test proves necessary:

- fund workspace and CRM import/manual intake;
- condition and evidence schema;
- permission controls;
- provenance, timestamps and version history;
- trigger inbox;
- response and decision log;
- human approval for every external action.

Evidence to seek before the EF Investment Committee:

- funds supplied real cases, not only interviews;
- conditions were specific enough to monitor;
- at least some satisfied triggers produced genuine reviews;
- a design partner continued using the workflow;
- at least one payer signed a pilot, paid, or made a credible operational commitment;
- a credible path exists from single-fund utility to multi-party coordination.

Do not invent fixed KPI thresholds in advance of observing base rates. Report the complete funnel and negative cases.

### FORM decision gate

Proceed only if the behavior is consequential. Pivot or stop if funds withhold real cases, conditions are mostly diplomatic, trigger events rarely alter decisions, or the product cannot earn workflow access without becoming a generic CRM feature.

## 6. EF months 4–6 — LAUNCH in San Francisco

LAUNCH should test whether the validated wedge can become a repeatable US-facing company rather than merely polish the prototype.

### Product development

- productionize tenant isolation, permissions, audit logs and deletion/retention controls;
- integrate with one dominant workflow surface rather than replacing the CRM;
- add configurable condition templates without forcing companies into universal metrics;
- support evidence-provider links and source-level provenance;
- measure recommendations, overrides and condition changes;
- keep models replaceable: the durable layer is state, rights, decisions and behavior.

### Commercial development

- convert design partners into paid annual pilots;
- select a narrow ICP based on observed usage, not prestige;
- test whether the payer values recovered opportunities, attention allocation, deployment timing or coordination;
- compare Europe and the US without assuming either is the final market;
- establish a repeatable founder/fund onboarding process;
- determine whether usage is frequent enough to support recurring SaaS.

### Network experiment

Only after single-tenant value exists, test a permissioned cross-party layer:

```text
Fund A records conditional lead interest
→ Founder authorizes limited disclosure
→ compatible Fund B is invited as follower
→ evidence/response is recorded
```

The test is whether one participant's state creates incremental value for another. A larger contact list is not a network effect.

### LAUNCH outcome sought

By the end of six months, the company should know whether it is:

1. a useful reconsideration/workflow SaaS;
2. a network business capable of coordinating conditional capital;
3. a feature better sold to or partnered with a CRM incumbent; or
4. an invalid thesis.

## 7. The 6–18 months after EF

### If the wedge works

1. Deepen the system of record for conditions, evidence and decisions.
2. Expand from reconsideration to active mandates and capital needs.
3. Introduce private lead/follower/lender dependencies.
4. Add a decomposed, contextual reliability record—never one universal public score.
5. Partner for KYC, signatures, cap-table and settlement rather than building everything immediately.
6. Enter execution only after specialist regulatory advice and clear customer demand.
7. Expand geographically based on density and cross-border demand, including the US; geography is a beachhead, not the moat.

### If the wedge is useful but not networked

Operate it as high-value B2B SaaS, integrate deeply with CRM/data-room systems and assess partnership or acquisition paths. Do not call normal SaaS retention a network effect.

### If the wedge fails but the broader problem remains

Test a different behavior, potentially active-mandate routing or diligence-attention allocation, using evidence from the failed experiment. Do not build the universal protocol without a behavior that naturally generates its edges.

## 8. Business model sequence

Initial model:

- capital provider pays a SaaS/pilot fee;
- startup participation is free or sponsor-funded;
- no public pay-to-rank mechanism;
- no transaction fee or success fee until regulatory implications are assessed.

Possible later revenue:

- fund and enterprise subscriptions;
- API/workflow infrastructure;
- permissioned network access;
- compliance and decision-record modules;
- transaction services only where authorized and strategically justified.

## 9. Competitive position

Axial and Dealsuite validate demand for private deal discovery and matching, but focus mainly on active M&A/capital-raising opportunities. Affinity and DealCloud own investor workflow and could add reconsideration. Carta, AngelList and private-market venues cover formation or execution once a transaction is concrete.

The intended distinction is:

```text
active deal listing and matching
versus
persistent capital state + conditional capacity + dependencies + observed response
```

This distinction is only meaningful if it changes behavior and produces unique data. Otherwise the product is a marketplace or CRM feature with different language.

## 10. North-star ambition and discipline

Google organized information, Amazon organized digital commerce and Meta digitized identity and relationships. The analogous ambition here is to organize the formation of private capital.

That comparison is a direction, not evidence. A credible path begins with one narrow behavior that users repeat, that produces a consequential edge automatically, and that improves coordination as participation grows.

```text
Vision: continuous infrastructure for private-capital formation
Wedge:  turn “not now” into a monitorable, permissioned decision
Asset:  condition–evidence–trigger–response history
Scale:  compose lead, follower, lender and company states
Moat:   workflow, permissions, provenance and behavioral graph
Gate:   real reconsiderations and multi-party value, not sign-ups
```

## 11. EF funding terms — working note

As publicly described by EF in July 2026:

- FORM London lasts 12 weeks.
- The £6,000 Talent Investment is equity-free personal support, not company funding.
- Teams passing EF's Investment Committee may receive **$125,000 through a post-money SAFE converting into 8% on a fully diluted basis**.
- EF advertises **up to $250,000 total** because LAUNCH can include a **further $125,000 investment** on separate published/program terms.
- Therefore, the correct shorthand is **$125k for 8%, plus a possible second $125k—not $250k for 8%**.
- The second tranche should not be treated as automatic. Confirm the applicable SAFE, Delaware C-Corp, LAUNCH and cohort-specific terms directly in the offer documents before relying on them.
